Setting up

TRAX plays the music already on your card, in the folders you keep it in — on a PSP, either PSP/MUSIC or MUSIC at the root. This section is the PSP; On a PS Vita covers the other machine.

Install

Copy the TRAX folder into PSP/GAME on the memory stick. TRAX then appears in the XMB under Game › Memory Stick.

PSP/
  GAME/
    TRAX/
      EBOOT.PBP          the app
      trax_kernel.prx    the helper: volume display, ♪ button, screen off
      SKINS/             cassette skins
  MUSIC/                 your music, in any folders you like

Music at the root of the card, in MUSIC, works just as well. That is the folder ARK-4 makes, and plenty of people use it.

Which folder TRAX reads

The first time it runs, TRAX looks in both. If only one has anything in it, that is the one it reads, without asking. If both have something, or neither does, it asks which to use and remembers the answer. Settings has a Music folder row to change it later.

The helper module

Some things only the PSP's system can see: the volume keys, the ♪ button, and turning the screen off. trax_kernel.prx lets TRAX use them. It needs custom firmware such as ARK-4, PRO or Adrenaline.

Without the helper, TRAX still plays everything. You won't get the on-screen volume, the ♪ button or Screen off while playing. The equalizer is still there two ways: hold for two thirds of a second, or hold SELECT for the visualization list, where it is the last entry.

On an unmodified PSP

A signed build of TRAX runs on firmware that has never been modified. It plays every format, draws every visualization and reads lyrics from the card. What it cannot do is anything needing the kernel — the volume display, the ♪ button and Screen off — or, usually, join a Wi-Fi network, since the PSP's WPA2 support comes from custom firmware.

Quitting

Press HOME as with any PSP app, or choose Quit on the Settings page.

On a PS Vita

TRAX has a native Vita build. It is the same player — the same list, cassette, equalizer and settings — drawn for the larger screen, and not the PSP version running under Adrenaline.

Install

Open TRAX.vpk with VitaShell and let it install. TRAX then appears on the home screen like any other application. Its own files live in ux0:/data/trax, which it makes itself: the settings, the log, lyrics it has fetched, and a SKINS folder for skins of your own.

Where the music goes

A Vita can have storage in three places at once, and TRAX reads any of them:

ux0:/music        the console's own storage
grw0:/MUSIC       a card in the game card slot — a microSD adapter
uma0:/MUSIC       the other card, where one is mounted

The first time it runs, TRAX looks in all three. If only one has anything in it, that is the one it reads, without asking. If more than one does, it asks which — offering only the folders that actually have music in them — and remembers the answer. Settings has a Music folder row to change it later, and that one lists every place the machine has, empty ones included, so you can point at a card before you have put anything on it.

The touchscreen

The front panel drags the progress bar: touch the dot and slide it, and the time beside it follows your finger rather than the decoder, so it does not lurch along behind. Everything else — the list, the menus, the visualizations — is buttons, exactly as on a PSP.

What differs from the PSP

  • No helper module, and none needed. The volume keys and the screen are the Vita's own business, and this machine has no ♪ button.
  • Lyrics work over Wi-Fi with nothing extra installed.
  • Skins are drawn for this screen. A Vita skin is 960x544, and its skin.json names pixels on a screen that size. See Cassette skins.
  • ATRAC3plus plays here too, through the Vita's own codec module.

The list

TRAX opens on the list of your music folder. The header shows the folder you're in, in bold, and how many items it holds. Folders come first, then tracks, each sorted by name.

Every row is one line. A long name is cut short with "…", keeping the file extension. Highlight it and it scrolls to show the whole name. Files TRAX can't play are listed, dimmed, rather than hidden.

Play modes

Choose the mode in the list, with L and R, before you start something. That way a folder can be set to shuffle and then started. The mode's name shows for a moment when you change it.

  • Play oncePlays from the chosen track to the end of the folder, then stops.
  • RepeatPlays the folder over and over.
  • Repeat onePlays the same track again each time it ends.
  • ShufflePlays every track once, in a random order.
  • Shuffle and repeatShuffles again each time round, and never starts a round with the track that just finished.
  • Global shufflePlays any track under your music folder, not just the ones in the folder you are looking at. See Global shuffle.

Whatever is playing keeps the mode it was started with. Changing the mode in the list affects only what you start next. The Player shows the queue's mode as a small icon beside the time. Play once has no icon.

Shuffling changes the order tracks play in, not the list. The folder still reads in its own order.

Standby

Tapping the power button puts the console into standby and TRAX with it. The music stops; tapping again wakes it and it carries on from where it was, in the same track at the same place.

TRAX cannot keep playing through standby, and this is worth being plain about rather than leaving you to wonder. An app playing with the console asleep has to hand its audio to the system's own player, which would mean giving up TRAX's decoders, its equalizer and the cassette's sense of where it is on the tape. Plugins exist that enable background playback for any app, installed into the console rather than into TRAX; if that is what you want, that is where it lives.

What TRAX does do is keep the console awake while it plays, so it will not suspend itself out from under an album. With Screen off while playing on, that means a dark screen and forty minutes of music.

Global shuffle

Every other mode plays the folder you are in. Global shuffle plays your whole music folder: any track, in any folder, in a random order.

It has to read your music folder before it can do that, and on a full card that takes a few seconds, so it asks first. Press on any track to start it, and TRAX will tell you what it is about to do:

Using global shuffle requires reading your entire music folder which may take time.

goes ahead. closes the dialog and nothing happens.

While it reads, a bar counts the folders at the top level of your music folder and says how many tracks it has found. cancels, and cancelling keeps nothing — part of a card is not a shuffle of it.

Things worth knowing:

  • Which track you press on makes no difference. The shuffle picks where to begin.
  • An album with a cue sheet contributes its tracks, not the one long file they live in.
  • It plays your card through once and stops. It does not go round again.
  • Stopping playback ends it, and the list of tracks it built is let go of there and then.
  • It is the one mode TRAX does not remember between launches. Every other mode is where you left it; this one is asked for each time, so that a press of out of habit never starts a read of your whole card.
  • Folders and albums still open normally while it is the chosen mode. Only pressing on a track starts it.

The bar counts top-level folders because that is the only total TRAX can know without reading your card twice. If your folders are very different sizes, expect it to move unevenly.

The headphone remote

With Sony's Remote Commander plugged in, its centre button pauses and resumes, and Forward and Back move through the queue as the shoulder buttons do. Its volume keys are the PSP's own.

The remote works from anywhere in TRAX — the list, a visualization, the settings page — and, more usefully, with the screen off: its buttons don't wake the screen, so an album can play through without the screen ever coming on. It works on an unmodified PSP too, where it is the only way to skip a track without looking.

Volume

The PSP's own volume keys work as usual. With the helper loaded, TRAX shows the level on screen while you change it.

Visualizations

Tap SELECT in the Player to swap to a visualization, and again to swap back. Hold SELECT to choose which one: to pick, to choose it, to close the list. TRAX remembers your choice.

Lyrics

The words live in a LYRICS folder at the root of the card, with your music's own folders mirrored inside it: a track at PSP/MUSIC/The Cars/Greatest Hits/album.flac keeps its words at LYRICS/The Cars/Greatest Hits/album.lrc. One folder, so the words are not mixed in among the music, and both builds of TRAX share whatever either has fetched. An .lrc sitting beside the track still works, and is no longer listed in the browser. A file with timings lights each line as it is sung; one without drifts through the track and says so. read back over what has been sung, and let go after five seconds. If the words are small for you, Settings has a Lyrics text size row: Default, Medium or Large.

fetches the words from lrclib.net over Wi-Fi and saves them into that folder, creating it as it goes. TRAX asks for an exact match first and, failing that, searches by name and takes the version closest in length — preferring one somebody has timed. A track of a cue album keeps its own file, album.03.lrc, since the album's tracks share one audio file.

Fetching needs a network the PSP can join, which in practice means custom firmware: WPA2 support comes from ARK-4's plugin, and unmodified firmware manages only WEP, WPA, or a router left in mixed WPA/WPA2 mode. Everything else about Lyrics works either way — including .lrc files you copy into LYRICS yourself — TRAX makes that folder the first time it runs, so it is there waiting.

Settings and About

Press START anywhere to open this page. Press it again or to close it. L and R switch between the two tabs.

Settings

pick a setting. or change it.

Music folder
PSP/MUSIC or MUSIC at the root of the card. Changing it reads the other folder straight away; anything playing carries on.
Lyrics text size
Default, Medium or Large: how big the words are drawn in the Lyrics visualization. Larger is easier to read from further away and fits fewer lines on the screen at once.
Sleep timer
Off, 15, 30, 60 or 90 minutes. The music fades out over the last half minute, then pauses. It's for one evening, so it's always Off when TRAX starts.
Screen off while playing
Off, or after 30 seconds, 1, 2 or 5 minutes without a button press. The screen uses most of the battery. Any button wakes it without doing anything else, and it comes back on by itself when the music stops. Needs the helper.
On a PS Vita this needs nothing extra, and TRAX now also keeps the machine from suspending itself while a track plays — so an album plays through with the screen dark rather than being cut off part way. Tapping the power button is different: that suspends TRAX, the music stops, and tapping again wakes it where it left off. See Standby.
Resume on launch
When on, TRAX opens the folder you were last playing from and puts the cursor on that track, remembering how far into it you were. Press and it carries on from there. It does not start playing by itself, and it does not open the file until you ask — which is what makes starting the app quick. Move the cursor or leave the folder and the remembered position is forgotten.
Quit
Press to leave TRAX.

About

Shows the version and build date, the battery level, the free space on the memory stick, whether the helper is running, which fonts are loaded, and credits for the libraries TRAX uses.

Cue albums

Some albums are one long audio file with a .cue sheet beside it that marks where each track starts. TRAX shows that pair as a single album, named from the sheet and with its cover as a thumbnail. The audio file and the picture don't get rows of their own.

  • on the album opens it like a folder, listing its tracks by number. The header shows the album's name after the folder.
  • on a track plays the album from that track. L and R in the Player move through the album.
  • goes back to the folder, with the album highlighted.

The time and progress bar show your place in the track, not in the whole file.

Cover art

TRAX uses the picture embedded in the track, as JPEG or PNG. If there isn't one, it uses a picture from the track's folder, looking in this order:

  • a picture named folder,
  • then one named album,
  • then one named after the folder itself,
  • and failing those, the first picture in the folder by name.

.jpg, .jpeg and .png all work, at any size. A large cover can take a few seconds to decode; a glowing white orb turns where it will appear, and the cover fades in over it. A track with no cover at all gets the orb in pink, purple and blue.

Cassette skins

The Tape deck's cassette is a picture from the SKINS folder — beside the app on a PSP, and in ux0:/data/trax/SKINS on a Vita. Add your own by giving each skin a folder:

SKINS/
  hub.png                    the reel hub every skin uses
  Maxell XLII 90/
    Maxell XLII 90.png       the picture: named after the folder, or skin.png
    skin.json                optional: where to print the title, and colors
    hub.png                  optional: this skin's own hub

Where the picture is transparent, the turning reels and tape show through. A skin without skin.json shows no text. In the Tape deck, step through the skins, and TRAX remembers the one you pick.

A skin is drawn for one screen. The positions in skin.json are pixels, so a PSP's skin is 480x272 and a Vita's is 960x544, and one file cannot serve both. The skins that ship are made for the machine they ship with; SKINS.html has the format.

Supported files

MP3
All common bit rates.
Opus
In .opus. Decoded at 48 kHz, with its tags and cover art.
FLAC
Including 24-bit at 96 kHz and 192 kHz.
WAV
8, 16, 24 and 32-bit PCM, 32-bit float, A-law and µ-law, and multichannel.
AAC
In .m4a, decoded by the PSP's own Media Engine.
ALAC
Apple Lossless, in .m4a.
ATRAC
ATRAC3 and ATRAC3plus, in .at3 — the PSP's own format, decoded by its Media Engine, and on a Vita by that machine's own codec module. Tracks bought from Sony's old Connect store are OpenMG-protected and will not play, on this or anything else.
Cue sheets
A .cue beside a single-file album. See Cue albums.

Names in accented Latin, Japanese and Korean display correctly. The fonts for Japanese and Korean load only when a name needs them.

In this beta

  • The list's bottom bar labels as Search, which is not built yet. goes up a folder, and carries no label because a list does that everywhere.
  • The volume display, the ♪ button and Screen off need the helper and custom firmware. None of the three applies on a Vita: that machine handles its own volume and screen, and has no ♪ button.
  • A very large progressive JPEG cover can take around ten seconds to appear while hi-res music plays on a PSP. The orb shows in the meantime.
  • On a Vita the touchscreen drags the progress bar and does nothing else yet; the list is still scrolled with the buttons.
  • On a Vita the skin that ships is the PSP's picture at half the size, standing in until one is drawn for the larger screen. Its text is placed for 960x544, so it sits oddly against the smaller picture.
  • uma0:/MUSIC is read but has never been tested, for want of a card that mounts there.
