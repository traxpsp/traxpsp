A music player for the PSP.

New in beta 3

Removed

  • Refresh (○) is gone from the list's bar. It had been labelled there since the first build and never wired up, because the rescan it promised turned out to be answering a question the PSP does not ask. Circle still goes up a level, which is what a list does everywhere and needs no label.

Added

  • ATRAC3 and ATRAC3plus in .at3, decoded on the Media Engine — the PSP's own format, and the one everything SonicStage ever put on a memory stick is in. An .at3 is a RIFF WAVE, so it arrives at the same branch a WAV does and the format tag decides; rather than match that tag against ATRAC3's two spellings, one of which hides inside an extensible header's GUID, TRAX offers anything it cannot make PCM of to the library and lets it answer. The library reads the file itself, so a track is fed from the card as it drains rather than held in memory.

    Tracks bought from Sony's Connect store are OpenMG-protected. Those cannot be played, by this or anything else, and nothing in TRAX can change that.

    Heard on a PSP-1000: three ATRAC3plus tracks open, stream and play, their lengths agreeing with what the files say to the millisecond, and a track change hands one decoder over to the next without either growing.

  • Lyrics text size, in Settings: Default, Medium or Large. The line being sung sets the scale and the lines around it follow in proportion, spacing with them, so the block keeps its shape at any of them — larger just means fewer lines on a screen 272 pixels tall. Asked for, and worth having: nobody reads a PSP at arm's length as easily as whoever picked the first size did.

New in beta 2

Added

  • Opus in .opus, tags and cover art included — a fifth format.
  • Lyrics, fetched by the PSP itself from lrclib.net over its own Wi-Fi. A visualization of their own: each line lights as it is sung, and Up and Down read back over what has been. They are kept in a LYRICS folder at the root of the card, with your music's own folders mirrored inside it.
  • VU meters: a pair of analog movements marked in VU and spaced by voltage as a real face is, with needles on a spring and a peak lamp.
  • A build for a PSP that was never modified. Signed, so official firmware will run it, and shipped beside the usual one as TRAX OFW. What it gives up is under Known limitations.
  • The headphone remote. Play, pause, forward and back from Sony's Remote Commander — on either firmware, and with the screen off, which is where a remote earns its keep.

Changed

  • Holding Select opens the list of visualizations whatever the firmware, and the equalizer is the last entry in it — so it can be reached where there is no ♪ button to read. A tap of Select now closes the equalizer; before, it did nothing there.
  • What the buttons do follows whether trax_kernel.prx loaded, not which build it is. With the helper, beta 1 exactly: ♪ opens the equalizer. Without it, holding △ for two thirds of a second does.
  • A release carries both builds, TRAX and TRAX OFW side by side, with the documents beside them rather than inside the app's own folder.
  • An .lrc is no longer listed in the browser, wherever it sits. There was never anything to be done with one there.

Fixed

  • Opus crashed on the first track. The decoding thread's stack was 16 KB, which suited every other format; Opus decodes through arrays on the stack and overran it. It is 64 KB now.
  • Opus froze the interface for two or three seconds as a track began, measuring the file a second time to read its tags — which on a memory stick means seeking to the end and working back. The tags are read from the front of the file instead, which brought Opus cover art with it.
  • Choosing the equalizer from the list of visualizations did nothing. The ✕ that chose it was seen again by the equalizer a moment later in the same frame, where ✕ means close, so it opened and shut before anything was drawn.
  • Holding Select on firmware with no helper opened the equalizer directly, leaving the list of visualizations — and so the Lyrics view — unreachable.
  • A popular song came back with “no lyrics” when lrclib had twenty versions of it. Every result in a search carries its words in full, so the reply ran past the half megabyte TRAX can hold, and what was left did not parse. A reply too big is now cut back to its last whole result rather than thrown away, and the log says that is what happened instead of blaming the server.
  • A track credited to more than one act found nothing. lrclib files “Dancing with Myself” under “Billy Idol” where the tag says “Billy Idol and Generation X”, and asking for the longer name returns not one result. Failing everything else, TRAX now asks by title alone and sorts the artist out itself — one name containing the other, and the length within ten seconds, since the server is no longer the one making sure it is the right act.

Features

Formats

  • MP3.
  • Opus in .opus, tags and cover art included.
  • FLAC, including 24-bit at 96 kHz and 192 kHz.
  • WAV: 8, 16, 24 and 32-bit PCM, 32-bit float, A-law, µ-law, and multichannel (extensible) files.
  • ALAC in .m4a, with Apple's reference decoder.
  • ATRAC3 / ATRAC3plus in .at3, decoded by the PSP's Media Engine. Not OpenMG: a track bought from Sony's Connect store will not play.
  • AAC in .m4a, decoded by the PSP's own Media Engine, so the main processor stays free.
  • Cue sheets. A single-file album with a .cue appears as one album, split into its tracks.
  • Seeking in every format.
  • Scrubbing that speeds up the longer it's held: normal speed, then 2× after 2 seconds and 3× after 5.

Browsing

  • Folders under ms0:/PSP/MUSIC or ms0:/MUSIC, whichever holds your music. At the first launch TRAX looks in both: it takes the one with something in it, and asks only when both are alike. Settings keeps the answer.
  • Folders come first, then tracks, each sorted by name.
  • Cue albums show as one entry, with their cover as a thumbnail, and open like a folder to their numbered tracks. Playing a track queues the whole album from there.
  • Track lengths, measured in the background while you browse.
  • One line per row. Long names are cut with "…" (keeping the file extension), and the selected row scrolls to show its full name.
  • A scrollbar in long folders, and a bold folder path above the list.
  • Accented, Japanese and Korean names. The Japanese and Korean fonts load only when a name needs them. A file whose long name the PSP can't reopen is opened by its short (8.3) name instead.

Playing

  • Five play modes, chosen with the shoulder buttons in the list: Play once, Repeat, Repeat one, Shuffle, and Shuffle and repeat.
  • In the shuffle modes, tapping ✕ starts on a random track, and holding it starts on the highlighted one.
  • Queues keep their own mode, so changing the mode in the list doesn't affect what's already playing.
  • A five-band equalizer (60 Hz to 12 kHz, ±12 dB) with five presets (Flat, Bass, Treble, Vocal, Loud), opened with the ♪ button. Your settings are saved.
  • The headphone remote. Sony's Remote Commander pauses, resumes and skips, from anywhere in TRAX and with the screen off — its buttons do not wake it. It needs no custom firmware.
  • An on-screen volume display when the volume keys are pressed. The PSP draws nothing over TRAX itself.

Player view

  • Cover art from JPEG and PNG, embedded in the file or found in the folder. It looks for folder, then album, then a picture named after the folder, then the first picture.
  • Large covers of any size, including 16 MB, 2,800-pixel PNGs.
  • While a slow cover decodes, a glowing white orb turns where it will appear, and the cover cross-fades in over it.
  • A track with no cover gets the orb in pink, purple and blue light.
  • A track information sidebar (△): format, codec, sample rate, bit depth and channels.
  • An icon beside the time shows how the queue is playing.
  • A battery level in the top left, filled to the charge: green while charging, red at 15% or below.

Visualizations

Tap Select to swap between the Player and your chosen visualization. Hold Select to choose one.

  • Tape deck: a cassette whose reels wind at physically correct speeds. It takes skins from SKINS, each a folder with a picture and an optional skin.json for label text and background colors.
  • Orb: the network orb reacting to the music, each latitude swelling with its own frequency band.
  • Level meter: stereo LED meters from −48 to 0 dB, with peak-hold markers.
  • Smoke: twisting ribbons of light that drift sideways and gust now and then.
  • Starfield: a calm flight through the stars.
  • Sphere matrix: 16 columns of shaded spheres, each column a frequency band, lighting up and springing larger with the music.
  • VU meters: a pair of analog movements, marked in VU and spaced by voltage as a real face is, with needles that take 300 ms to the mark and step a little past it.
  • Lyrics: each line lighting as it is sung, with Up and Down to read back. Triangle fetches them from lrclib.net over Wi-Fi — an exact match first, then a search for the closest version somebody has timed. They are kept in a LYRICS folder at the root of the card, mirroring the music's own folders, so the words are not mixed in among the music and both builds share what either has fetched. An .lrc beside the track still works, and is no longer listed in the browser.
  • The track title fades in over a visualization when it starts and on each track change.

Settings and About (Start)

  • Lyrics text size: Default, Medium or Large for the words in the Lyrics visualization.
  • Sleep timer: 15 to 90 minutes, fading out over the last half minute, then pausing.
  • Screen off while playing: after 30 seconds to 5 minutes without a press. Any button wakes the screen, and it also relights when the music stops. The screen is where most of the battery goes.
  • Resume on launch: reopens the last track, paused where you left it.
  • About: version, build date, battery, memory stick free space, helper module status, loaded fonts, and credits for the libraries TRAX uses.

Look and feel

  • Smooth 12-pixel icons for the PSP's buttons and the play modes, anti-aliased rather than pixel-plotted.
  • Text drawn at the fonts' native size, pixel for pixel like the PSP's own menus.
  • TRAX name and icon in the XMB.

Engineering improvements

Memory

  • The main thread's stack no longer overflows. The play queue (about 190 KB) and cue-sheet structures (about 32 KB) had been on its 256 KB stack. On a real PSP that ended in a black screen; the emulator never enforced it.
  • Covers are scaled down as they decode, instead of being decoded full size first.
    • A 1,500-pixel PNG used to need 8.6 MB and now needs only the thumbnail.
    • A 16.7 MB, 2,843-pixel cover now decodes in 1.8 MB, where it couldn't be opened at all before.
    • Covers stream from the file rather than being loaded into memory first, so there's no size limit.
  • Skipping stops a cover's decode. A progressive JPEG holds up to 12 MB for the ten seconds it decodes. When you skip, that memory is released before the next file opens.
  • The Japanese and Korean fonts load only when needed, leaving about 3 MB more free for most music libraries.
  • ALAC buffers are sized correctly. One track asked for 2.3 MB instead of 30 KB, which failed once memory was fragmented.
  • Skins are built before the old one is released, so a failed load no longer leaves nothing on screen.
  • No leaks. Damaged covers had leaked everything decoded so far: 38 MB over 5,000 test images. A 5-hour-52-minute soak test (468 tracks, every visualization) ended with the heap exactly as it started, and heap use held at 3.6–4.1 MB throughout.
  • Size fields from files are capped, so a damaged header can't ask for an absurd buffer or wrap round to a tiny one on the PSP's 32-bit system.

Crash and safety fixes

Most of these were found by fuzzing: millions of deliberately damaged files run through the file readers on the Mac with AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer.

  • Mono AAC overran a buffer by 4 KB.
  • Damaged MP4 box sizes near 4 GB slipped past the bounds checks, and the reader then read far past the end of the file's index. The same pattern was fixed in the MP4 sample tables and in the ALAC configuration.
  • Apple's ALAC decoder trusted its input in six ways: sample counts, channel counts, and reads past the end of a packet. All six are fixed and marked in the source.
  • An MP3 tag with an empty text field crashed the app.
  • FLAC comment lengths could wrap round. Float WAVs containing NaN produced undefined samples.
  • A 1 KB fake JPEG claiming 65,000 pixels a side asked for gigabytes of memory.
  • Oversized PNG dimensions in covers, skins and icons could wrap round to a small allocation.
  • Bad directory data from the memory stick caused an exception; it's now checked before use.
  • File paths are only used when they fit their buffers whole. The build now has no compiler warnings.

Bug fixes

  • Accented letters drew as the wrong character, for example "Río" came out with a capital I. The cause was a font caching option.
  • Some files with non-ASCII names couldn't be opened; they now open by their short name.
  • The cassette flickered on real hardware. Four causes were fixed: a stale graphics texture cache, a frame being shown while still being drawn (fixed with triple buffering), sprites on fractional pixels, and textures too wide for the graphics chip's cache.
  • PNG covers didn't work.
  • AAC was refused entirely, and once it played, its first second was garbled.
  • ALAC tracks sometimes failed with "Couldn't play" after several tracks.
  • FLAC seeking failed through the new block reader.
  • The equalizer closed the whole player instead of just itself, and didn't save changes made with Up/Down.
  • 16-bit FLAC and WAV are now bit-exact. Every sample had been scaled down by a hair.
  • Long names spilled into the row below, and list text was slightly blurred from being drawn at 0.9 size.
  • A repeated track's cover blinked on every loop.
  • Opus crashed, then froze the interface for seconds at each track: a 16 KB decoder stack it decodes past, and a second pass over the file to read tags.
  • The equalizer could not be chosen from the list of visualizations: the press that chose it was read again as the press that closes it.
  • The top of the cover now and then showed half drawn for a frame. The display was picking up the next frame while it was still being drawn; buffers now change only at a vertical sync.

Speed

  • Opening an MP3 no longer reads the whole file. Finding a tag at the very end was taking 2.3 seconds of the 2.55 seconds needed to open a 9 MB track.
  • Streaming reads the card in 128 KB aligned blocks instead of 1 KB pieces.
    • A 24-bit/192 kHz FLAC had been 630 card reads a second.
    • On one card, misaligned reads took 123 ms per block against 8 ms aligned.
    • Hi-res FLAC now plays cleanly even at a 222 MHz processor speed.
  • Covers decode on their own low-priority thread, and step aside whenever the audio buffer dips.
  • A cover is read from the card in the gap between tracks, not during playback. That took underruns from 50 to 0.
  • Cue album covers are cached after the first decode. The Technotronic cover took 15 seconds on every play before.
  • Track lengths are read from the file handles the folder scan already opens.
  • Drawing:
    • Circles use 4.1× fewer vertices.
    • The skin is drawn in strips that fit the graphics chip's 8 KB texture cache.
    • Icons are rendered once at startup.
    • Each visualization element is one batched draw call.
    • Sines come from a lookup table.
  • AAC output is read through the cached view of memory; reading through the uncached one cost more than decoding.
  • The music analysis runs only while a visualization is showing, below the decoder's priority.

ATRAC

  • The second buffer. sceAtrac refuses every seek with NEED_SECOND_BUFFER until it has been handed a slice from near the end of the track, held apart from the streaming one so it can reach the end without the ring having got there. It says where and how much — 376 bytes for a 57-second track — and a track short enough not to need one says so instead. Playing worked without it; only scrubbing gave the game away.
  • Nothing to discard at the start. AAC arrives with 2,112 samples of encoder run-up that have to be dropped. ATRAC has none: the first frame off the Media Engine is already music. The emulator suggested otherwise — it returns near-silence there — and following it would have taken 27 ms off the front of every track, quietly.
  • The format tag is not worth matching. An .at3 is a RIFF WAVE, so it lands where a WAV does, but ATRAC3plus hides behind an extensible header whose SubFormat GUID reads as tag 0xAABF under the usual “first two bytes” rule, and declares 0 bits per sample. Anything wav_parse cannot make PCM of is offered to the library instead, which knows what it can decode.

Networking

Fetching the words for a track is the first thing TRAX does over the air, and none of it was a given on a 333 MHz machine.

  • TLS 1.2 on the PSP itself. lrclib.net is HTTPS only. It offers ECDHE-ECDSA with ChaCha20-Poly1305, which is the one modern suite this processor is happy with: ChaCha is arithmetic it can do, and an ECDSA certificate is far cheaper to check than an RSA one. The trust roots are compiled in rather than taking whatever answers — a player that skipped that would take words, or anything else, from whoever else was on the Wi-Fi.
  • Three megabytes are kept back from the heap, because the heap had been taking the whole partition and the system's network modules then had nowhere to load: 220 KB free, and the module refused. The log's opening line now says how much is outside the heap, so this cannot go quietly wrong again.
  • The PSP's clock is read directly. newlib's time() never reaches the hardware clock — it answers as though it were 1970, so every valid certificate “starts in the future” and was refused, on a PSP whose own clock was right.
  • Chunked replies are reassembled before anything tries to read them. Cloudflare sends this API's answers as a series of lengths with data between them, and handing that to a JSON parser looked exactly like “no lyrics for this track”.
  • A busy server is retried three times, two seconds apart, and named as such: lrclib answers 503 often enough to be worth telling apart from a fault.
  • An album's tracks each keep their own file. They share one audio file, so they had all written to one .lrc, each fetch overwriting the last, and the times then belonged to a different track.

Testing and tools

  • A fuzzer for every file reader under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer (tools/fuzz), with leak checks.
  • Decodes are bit-exact against ffmpeg, checked for FLAC, WAV and ALAC.
  • A soak-test build (TRAX SOAK) that presses buttons by itself for hours and logs memory and thread stacks.
  • A visualization preview renderer (tools/vispreview) that renders any visualization to video with a track's sound.

Known limitations

  • Search (△) is labelled in the list's bar but not implemented yet.
  • On a PSP that was never modified — the TRAX OFW build — four things are missing, and none of them can be worked around in software:
    • No Wi-Fi, so no fetching lyrics. The PSP's WPA2 support comes from ARK-4's plugin; official firmware manages only WEP, WPA, or a router left in mixed WPA/WPA2 mode. Everything else about Lyrics works, including .lrc files you copy into LYRICS yourself.
    • No screen off while playing, which is where most of the battery goes.
    • No volume display.
    • No ♪ button. Volume, ♪, screen and hold can only be read in kernel mode, and signed homebrew is user mode only. Holding △ for two thirds of a second opens the equalizer instead.
  • With custom firmware, those three need trax_kernel.prx beside the EBOOT. Without it TRAX still runs, and behaves as it does on official firmware.
  • Mono AAC has been fixed and fuzzed but not yet heard on a PSP.
  • Plain ATRAC3 has not been heard on a PSP either. Every sample to hand was ATRAC3plus, and the two go through one code path, but that is an argument rather than a test.
  • Scrubbing an ATRAC track has been proven on the emulator and not yet on hardware. It is the part the second buffer exists for, so it is the part worth trying first.
  • A large progressive JPEG cover is still the tightest moment for memory: about 2.3 MB free at its peak, with the Japanese font loaded.
  • Sony's fonts (ltn8.pgf, jpn0.pgf) must not be included in a distributed build. A PSP loads them from its own system storage.
